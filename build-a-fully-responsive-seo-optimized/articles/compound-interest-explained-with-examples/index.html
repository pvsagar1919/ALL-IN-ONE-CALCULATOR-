Compound Interest Explained With Examples

See how time, rate, frequency, and monthly contributions shape long-term growth.

Compounding rewards time

Compound interest earns interest on previous interest. The longer money stays invested, the more growth comes from past growth instead of only new contributions.

Frequency matters, but rate matters more

Monthly or daily compounding can improve returns slightly compared with annual compounding. The annual rate and time period usually have the bigger effect.

Contributions change the curve

Regular monthly additions create a growing stream of deposits. Each deposit compounds for its own remaining period, which is why SIP and contribution formulas differ from a single lump sum.
